\begin{abstract}
In industrial fault diagnosis, the scarcity of fault samples presents a significant challenge for training effective machine learning models. While data augmentation techniques have been widely adopted to address class imbalance, existing approaches rely on fixed strategies that fail to adapt to the underlying signal characteristics. This paper proposes a \emph{joint optimization} framework that simultaneously selects optimal feature subsets and resampling strategies based on signal-specific statistical features. Through comprehensive analysis of the strategy-feature coupling matrix, we discover that entropy features (sample entropy, permutation entropy) consistently outperform full feature sets across all strategies, achieving 15-21\% improvement in F1-macro. A lightweight decision tree recommendation engine maps signal meta-features to optimal (strategy, feature-set) pairs. The proposed method achieves comparable performance to the oracle while being 100,000x faster than Auto-sklearn, and demonstrates generalization capability in cross-dataset validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Rotating machinery is the backbone of modern industrial systems, and bearing failures can lead to catastrophic equipment damage and production losses. Early fault detection through vibration signal analysis has gained widespread attention in predictive maintenance. However, a fundamental challenge persists: normal operational data vastly outnumbers fault samples in real-world scenarios. This class imbalance problem severely degrades the performance of supervised learning algorithms, causing models to be biased toward the majority class.

Data augmentation techniques, particularly resampling methods, have emerged as effective solutions for addressing class imbalance. Techniques such as Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE) and its variants generate synthetic minority samples to balance the training set. However, a critical limitation underlies all existing approaches: they apply a \emph{fixed} resampling strategy regardless of the input signal's characteristics. The choice between SMOTE, Borderline-SMOTE, ADASYN, or hybrid approaches is typically made based on empirical experience rather than systematic analysis.

More critically, we observe that the \emph{optimal feature set} is tightly coupled with the choice of resampling strategy. Using all features does not necessarily yield the best classification performance. Our analysis reveals that entropy features consistently outperform full feature sets, but the optimal strategy varies with the imbalance ratio (IR). This discovery motivates our joint optimization framework.

The main contributions of this paper are threefold:

\begin{itemize}
\item We discover and analyze the \emph{strategy-feature coupling phenomenon}: different resampling strategies prefer different feature subsets, and this coupling varies with the imbalance ratio. This insight fundamentally changes how we approach resampling strategy selection.

\item We propose a joint optimization framework that simultaneously selects optimal feature subsets and resampling strategies based on signal meta-features. Rather than first choosing a strategy then applying it to all features, our approach recommends the optimal (strategy, feature-set) pair for each input signal.

\item We conduct comprehensive experiments on the CWRU bearing dataset and validate generalization through cross-dataset experiments (CWRU trained $\rightarrow$ MFPT tested). Our method achieves 15-21\% improvement over fixed-strategy baselines and reaches 97-100\% of the oracle performance.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Imbalance Learning for Fault Diagnosis}
Class imbalance is a pervasive challenge in fault diagnosis. Traditional approaches include random undersampling (RUS) of the majority class and oversampling of the minority class. SMOTE generates synthetic samples by interpolating between minority class samples and their k-nearest neighbors. Extensions such as Borderline-SMOTE focus on samples near the decision boundary, while ADASYN adaptively adjusts sampling density based on local class density. Hybrid methods like SMOTE+ENN combine oversampling with cleaning of boundary samples.

\subsection{Feature-Resampling Interaction}
While feature selection has been extensively studied in machine learning, its interaction with resampling strategies remains underexplored. Chawla et al. noted that SMOTE's interpolation in high-dimensional space can produce noisy samples. He et al. proposed adaptive sampling based on local density estimation. Batista et al. analyzed the behavior of several resampling methods but treated feature selection as a separate stage.

\textbf{Central observation:} The optimal feature subset depends on which resampling strategy is used. For instance, Borderline-SMOTE with entropy features achieves F1=0.80, but with all features drops to 0.63 --- a 21\% degradation. Conversely, SMOTE with entropy features achieves F1=0.86, its best performance across all feature sets.

To the best of our knowledge, this paper is the first to: (1) systematically construct a strategy-feature coupling matrix across multiple imbalance ratios, and (2) propose joint optimization of feature subset and resampling strategy.

\subsection{Domain-Specific Meta-Features}
Prior AutoML work (e.g., Auto-sklearn) employs generic meta-features such as sample count, feature dimensionality, and imbalance ratio. These dataset-level statistics do not capture the physical characteristics of the signal itself.

In contrast, our meta-features are domain-specific and physically interpretable: RMS reflects bearing vibration energy, spectral entropy quantifies frequency distribution complexity, and permutation entropy captures signal regularity. These features directly relate to the physical mechanisms of fault generation in rotating machinery, enabling more meaningful strategy selection than generic meta-features.

\textbf{Distinction from generic meta-learning:} Our method is fundamentally different from generic meta-learning or recommendation systems in three aspects:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem formulation:} Generic meta-learning recommends model configurations (e.g., learning rate, architecture) for any dataset. Our joint optimization targets a \emph{signal-specific phenomenon}: the strategy-feature coupling that exists only in vibration signal classification.
    \item \textbf{Meta-feature design:} Generic meta-learning uses abstract statistical features (e.g., data entropy, mutual information). Our meta-features have explicit physical meanings tied to bearing fault mechanisms (e.g., RMS $\leftrightarrow$ vibration energy, permutation entropy $\leftrightarrow$ signal regularity).
    \item \textbf{Model complexity:} Rather than neural network-based meta-learners, our recommender uses a shallow decision tree (depth $\leq 6$) that learns interpretable rules like ``if permutation\_entropy $<$ 0.7 and IR $>$ 5, then Borderline-SMOTE + entropy''.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{AutoML for Pipeline Selection}
Recent advances in Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) have explored pipeline selection and hyperparameter optimization. Tools like Auto-sklearn employ Bayesian optimization to search for optimal preprocessing and classification pipelines. However, these approaches are designed for generic tabular data and do not leverage domain-specific signal features. Furthermore, the computational cost of AutoML methods makes them unsuitable for real-time industrial applications.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Problem Definition}
Given an imbalanced signal dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}, y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ with imbalance ratio $IR = n_0 / n_1$, our goal is to simultaneously recommend:
\begin{itemize}
    \item An optimal resampling strategy $s^* \in \{SMOTE, B-SMOTE, ADASYN, RUS, SMOTETomek\}$
    \item An optimal feature subset $f^* \in \{\text{time}, \text{freq}, \text{entropy}, \text{time+freq}, \text{time+entropy}, \text{freq+entropy}, \text{all}\}$
\end{itemize}
such that the pair $(s^*, f^*)$ maximizes classification F1-macro.

\subsection{Signal Preprocessing}
Raw vibration signals are segmented into frames of 1024 samples with 50\% overlap. A Hanning window is applied to each frame to reduce spectral leakage. DC components are removed through detrending.

\subsection{Strategy-Feature Coupling Analysis}
We construct a $5 \times 7$ \emph{coupling matrix} $M$ where $M_{s,f}$ represents the F1-macro achieved by strategy $s$ using feature set $f$. The analysis reveals:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Entropy dominance}: For all strategies, using \emph{entropy features only} (2 dimensions) achieves higher F1 than using all 12 features. This suggests that time-domain and frequency-domain features introduce noise for this classification task.
    \item \textbf{Strategy-specific optimal feature sets}: While entropy is universally beneficial, different strategies have different secondary preferences. Borderline-SMOTE shows the largest performance drop when using non-entropy features.
    \item \textbf{IR-dependent coupling}: The coupling strength varies with the imbalance ratio. At IR=3, the entropy advantage is smallest; at IR=10, it is most pronounced.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Joint Recommendation Engine}
Based on the coupling analysis, we design a decision tree that recommends not just a strategy, but a (strategy, feature-set) pair. The decision tree is trained on meta-features augmented with the IR value as an additional input feature. Strategy labels are generated by selecting the pair that maximizes $M_{s,f}$ for each training sample.

\textbf{Why a shallow decision tree instead of neural meta-learners?} Generic meta-learning systems (e.g., LSTM-based model recommenders, graph neural networks for pipeline search) require thousands of meta-training tasks to generalize. Our coupling matrix reveals a remarkably simple structure: entropy features dominate across all strategies, and the remaining variation is captured by IR-dependent rules. A depth-6 decision tree is sufficient to capture these patterns while remaining interpretable and computationally lightweight (3ms inference vs. 300s for Auto-sklearn).

\subsection{Dataset Description}
Experiments are conducted on the Case Western Reserve University (CWRU) bearing dataset (4 classes: Normal, B, IR, OR; 1150 samples, 12 features). To simulate varying degrees of class imbalance, we construct scenarios with IR = 3, 5, and 10 by subsampling the majority class (Normal=150) while preserving minority class samples. Cross-dataset validation uses the MFPT bearing dataset (3 classes: Normal, Inner, Outer; 750 samples).

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{The Strategy-Feature Coupling Phenomenon}
Our key finding is that optimal feature set depends on the resampling strategy. Entropy features excel because they capture the \emph{complexity} and \emph{regularity} of fault patterns, which directly relates to how synthetic samples should be generated. Time-domain features, while physically meaningful, introduce noise that confuses the resampling process.

This coupling has important practical implications: simply choosing the best strategy while using all features is suboptimal. The joint optimization framework addresses this by considering both dimensions simultaneously.

\subsection{Why Does the Recommender Work?}
The effectiveness stems from two factors: (1) the coupling matrix reveals that entropy features dominate, reducing the decision space; (2) the decision tree learns IR-dependent strategy selection rules that generalize across datasets.

At IR=3, the joint recommender outperforms because it dynamically selects Borderline-SMOTE for low-entropy signals, while at higher IR it correctly defaults to SMOTE+entropy.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
Several limitations should be acknowledged:

\begin{itemize}
\item The CWRU and MFPT datasets share similar fault types (inner/outer race), which may inflate cross-dataset performance. Validation on more diverse datasets is needed.
\item Our current feature grouping is coarse (time/freq/entropy). Finer-grained analysis of individual features may reveal additional coupling patterns.
\item The formulation is evaluated on 4-class classification. Extension to more classes and higher imbalance ratios remains future work.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Relationship to Generic Meta-Learning}
Our framework is distinct from generic meta-learning recommendation systems in both motivation and methodology:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scope:} Generic meta-learners (e.g., Auto-sklearn's Bayesian optimization, neural architecture search) search across a vast space of model configurations for arbitrary tasks. Our method addresses a \emph{specific coupling phenomenon} unique to vibration signal classification: the finding that entropy features consistently outperform full features for all resampling strategies.
    \item \textbf{Training data:} Generic meta-learning requires meta-tasks drawn from diverse domains (image classification, NLP, tabular data). Our training data comes from a single mechanism: the strategy-feature coupling matrix derived from CWRU bearing signals. This narrow but deep signal-specific knowledge is what enables 100,000x speedup over Auto-sklearn.
    \item \textbf{Interpretability:} The decision tree rules (e.g., ``permutation\_entropy $<$ 0.7 $\Rightarrow$ Borderline-SMOTE'') have direct physical meaning for bearing fault diagnosis. This interpretability is critical for industrial adoption, where domain experts need to validate and trust the recommendations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
This paper proposed a joint optimization framework for feature selection and resampling strategy recommendation in industrial fault diagnosis. Our key discovery is the strategy-feature coupling phenomenon: entropy features consistently outperform full feature sets across all strategies, but the optimal strategy varies with the imbalance ratio.

The joint recommendation engine, based on a lightweight decision tree, achieves 97-100\% of the oracle performance and outperforms fixed strategies by 15-21\%. Cross-dataset validation confirms generalization capability.

Future work will explore finer-grained feature analysis, larger-scale datasets, and online deployment with concept drift adaptation.
